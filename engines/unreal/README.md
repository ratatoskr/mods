# Unreal

For games made with Unreal Engine 4 or 5.

- Finds the engine's names, objects and class layouts as the game starts. It works them out from what every game has, so it doesn't need signatures or offsets for each engine version.
- Wraith's free camera moves the player's camera, cutscenes included, so the free camera and orbiting work without a plugin for the game. It goes in as a camera modifier, which the engine runs after the game's own camera code. Photo mode lists the cameras: the game's own, every camera actor in the level, cine cameras included, and any you add where the view is. With Follow selection on, the view comes from the one picked, without touching the game's own view target. Moving a level camera with the free camera doesn't move the actor, so it's back where the level has it when you come back to it. Added cameras stay where you left them, and they're views kept by the plugin rather than actors, so nothing's left in the world once it stops. Renaming a level camera only lasts till the game closes, since Unreal's names can't change.
- Posing poses the 32 animated characters nearest the camera, as each one's animated and before what it holds or wears is placed, so weapons and accessories follow edits, as do clothes and hair that follow its pose. While it's open those characters are animated in full every frame, at their most detailed, and while the world's paused they keep being animated in place, so posing works the same, and nothing else moves however long it's held. Skeletons over 512 bones get their first 512. Their meshes' morph targets, and those of meshes that follow them, like a face on a body, go to posing as blend shapes too.
- Unreal's game thread drives Wraith's ticks, so other plugins' `threads.onTick` runs there, once a frame, where calling into the engine is safe.
- Wraith's game speed goes through the world's time dilation, so only the world changes and the UI carries on. 0 pauses the world the engine's own way, so nothing creeps however long you pose, and the player's camera is kept going through it, so the free camera and posing still work. The game's own slow motion still works underneath it.
- Games that ship without a console get one back, opened with the game's console key, usually ` or ~. The Unreal console window, under the plugin's button on the overlay's bar, runs commands too, for games with no console key.
- Asks the game's settings whether HDR's on, now and then, so Wraith's overlay matches the game even when Wraith joined the game late.
- The Workbench gets an Unreal tab, with Scene and Classes in it. Scene lists the current world's levels and their actors, with attached actors under what they're attached to. Pick one to see its components and properties, read through the engine's reflection, and change numbers, bools, enums and vectors in place. Hide or show actors, orbit the free camera round them, copy them, or call any of their functions that take nothing. Classes lists every class, struct and enum, with their properties' offsets and their functions, and finds a live one of a class for Scene.
- Writes every class and struct, with their properties' offsets and types and their functions, to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Wraith\dumps\unreal\<game id>.txt`, about 10 seconds after it finds them.
- Gives other plugins, Workbench scripts and tweaks the `Unreal` service, in `schemas/unreal.sal`. It finds objects by class, reads and sets their properties by name, calls their functions, and moves actors. It works on the game thread only, so use `threads.onTick` or a script on the game thread.
